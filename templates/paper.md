# Paper Note

## 1. Source and reading boundary

Record the inspected version, available artifacts and unread material.

## 2. Problem and evaluation setting

Specify the task, physical system, operating conditions and objective.

## 3. Testable claims

Distinguish author claims, observations and reader interpretation.

## 4. Contribution and comparison class

Identify changed components and the prior work actually compared.

## 5. System and information flow

Trace observations, states, actions, supervision and parameter updates.

## 6. Proposed mechanism

Connect the intervention, intermediate property and measured outcome.

## 7. Physical dynamics and computation

If relevant, map substrate, excitation, sensing, temporal state and readout.

## 8. Self-model or body representation

If relevant, define represented quantities, coordinates and adaptation tests.

## 9. Data and learning procedure

Record splits, preprocessing, optimization, selection and leakage risks.

## 10. Baselines and controls

Compare inputs, supervision, resources and tuning conditions.

## 11. Quantitative observations

Record source locators, metrics, units, denominator and uncertainty.

## 12. Discriminating tests

Map ablations and interventions to the explanations they can distinguish.

## 13. Generalization boundary

Separate in-distribution, OOD, task/body transfer and hardware evidence.

## 14. Failures and limitations

Keep reported failures distinct from concerns inferred by the reader.

## 15. Reproduction requirements

Record actual artifact availability and missing implementation information.

## 16. Claim-to-source map

For each important claim, retain its evidence label, locator and source trail.

## 17. Research implications

State the bounded next reading or test without declaring an unreviewed gap.
